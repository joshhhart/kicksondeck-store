Which Yeezy Silhouette Should You Buy? 350 V2 vs Foam Runner vs Slides

Kicks on Deck · July 29, 2026 · 5 min read

Three silhouettes, one rotation, and a lot of people asking the same question before they buy their first pair: 350 V2, Foam Runner, or Slides — which one actually fits how you live? We've compared 350 V2 vs Foam Runner and Foam Runner vs Slides separately, but nobody's put all three side by side in one place. Until now.

Short version: the 350 V2 is the sneaker, the Foam Runner is the do-anything slip-on, and the Slide is the shoe you don't think about. Here's how to know which one is your first pair.

The 350 V2 — the everyday sneaker

This is the one that started the rotation for most people. Primeknit upper, boost midsole, low profile, laces. It reads as an actual sneaker in a way the other two don't, which means it goes further outside the house — work, travel, dates, anywhere a clog or a slide would look like you gave up. The Beluga 2.0 is one of the most balanced colorways in the lineup, grey-and-orange enough to stand out without trying too hard.

Trade-off: it's a lace-up, so it's not a grab-and-go shoe, and boost needs real care (see the 350 V2 cleaning guide) if you don't want it yellowing. Full breakdown of fit in the 350 V2 sizing guide and every color option in the 350 V2 colorways guide.

Pick the 350 V2 if: you want one shoe that works for both "leaving the house" and "actual outfit."

The Foam Runner — the do-anything slip-on

One piece of molded EVA foam, ventilation holes, real tread, and a closed toe. It's the only one of the three you can genuinely run errands in — grocery store, gas station, short walks — without your foot sliding around. The Ararat colorway is a neutral earth tone that pairs with almost anything, which matters since Foam Runners are a statement shoe whether you want them to be or not.

Sizing is the catch: Foam Runners run big for most feet, and the Foam Runner sizing guide covers exactly how much to size down. Full color lineup is in the Foam Runner colorways guide.

Pick the Foam Runner if: you want the most comfortable shoe you own and don't mind it being the loudest thing in the room.

The Slide — the shoe you don't think about

No laces, no break-in, no decisions. A soft footbed and a single strap. The Earth Brown is the quietest colorway in the lineup — the one you can wear with sweats to the mailbox and not think twice about. Slides aren't trying to be a real outfit; they're the shoe you put on when you've already decided not to try.

They also run big — check the Yeezy Slides sizing guide before you order — and every colorway is mapped out in the Slides colorways guide.

Pick Slides if: you want the lowest-effort shoe in your rotation and you're not walking further than the driveway.

Which Yeezy silhouette should I buy first?

If you're only buying one pair, get the 350 V2. It's the most versatile of the three — the only one that convincingly works as an actual outfit shoe, not just a house shoe. Foam Runners and Slides are both excellent second and third pairs once you already have a sneaker in rotation, since neither one covers you for situations that call for laces.

Can the fit quiz tell me which silhouette to buy?

Yes — the 60-second fit quiz asks about how you'll wear the shoe (errands vs. outfit vs. lounging) and your usual size, then points you at a specific silhouette and colorway instead of making you guess from three guides. It's the fastest way to skip this whole comparison if you just want an answer.

So which one wins?

There's no universal winner — that's the whole point of having three silhouettes instead of one. Cop the 350 V2 first if you want a real sneaker, add the Foam Runner when you want something you can wear anywhere without laces, and grab Slides once you just want a shoe you never have to think about. Most people who build out the full rotation end up owning all three for exactly that reason.
